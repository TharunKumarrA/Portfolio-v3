Life, like physics, often plays by laws unseen. Newton’s First Law of Motion says a body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion stays in motion, unless acted upon by an external force. This means potential energy alone doesn’t make things move — momentum does. A boulder sitting at the edge of a cliff, full of potential energy, won’t move until something pushes it. Only when momentum kicks in does that energy turn into motion, direction, and impact. And isn’t life a lot like that?

Everyone carries within them a kind of potential energy, skills, dreams, and visions of who they could be. But unless there’s movement, unless there’s consistent action, it all remains stored, unused, and eventually forgotten. Society loves potential. It praises the “smart one,” the “promising student,” the one who could achieve great things. But what use is potential that never meets reality?

Often, there’s comfort in knowing potential exists. It becomes a shield, a place to hide. If the effort isn’t fully made, failure can be explained away with, “I didn’t give it my best.” This subtle trick keeps the ego intact. The fear of falling short despite giving it all, the most human fear of all, leads to hesitation, excuses, and illusions of progress through thought and talk alone.

Yet life rewards not the ones who could have, but the ones who did. The ones who showed up every single day, those who kept moving even when nothing made sense, even when doubt whispered louder than hope. That’s momentum, not loud, not flashy, but persistent. Momentum builds character, it builds direction, it makes people who were once average into forces that shape their world.

There’s a critical difference between dreaming of being a writer and writing every morning before the world wakes up, between imagining fitness and showing up to train when it rains, between wanting to learn and doing the boring reps of practice that no one sees. Potential sits, momentum moves, and in that motion, identity forms.

Sometimes, the hardest truths come not from others but from mirrors, silent, honest, unavoidable. It’s easy to romanticize change or inspiration, but real growth often comes from confronting patterns of escape, facing the harsh reality of distractions, excuses, and delayed effort. It becomes clear that many goals once chased were not true desires, but veils, fancy titles or distant dreams used to cover the inability to commit to the hard things in front.

Phones, reels, endless scrolling, they steal not just time but attention, and with it, the ability to focus, to learn, to create. It’s not sin or weakness, it’s a habit. Like any habit, it can be unlearned, but not through guilt or grand resolutions, through daily action, through choosing not to run, through remembering that what one becomes is not tied to what they once were but what they consistently choose now.

Life is short, but hours are long, and every long hour spent in silent effort away from noise is a brick laid in a foundation that actually holds, not castles in the sky, but homes built with truth.

Potential is a beautiful thing, but momentum — momentum is what rewrites lives.
