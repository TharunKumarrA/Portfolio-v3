The whole of India witnessed the historic launch of Chandrayaan 3 belted tight to the GSLV Mark III rocket on July 14th at 2:35 PM on its way to the moon. I, too, was among the billion eyes that eagerly watched the live launch from my hostel room, forgetting the pressure of the end-sem exam for a brief moment as the rocket soared into the sky, filling me and my friends with a sense of pride.

Now as our beloved spacecraft is just weeks away from its successful landing on the moon, this article is aimed at answering the question of why ISRO exists and a recap of the humble beginnings of ISRO and how it grew to become one of the main driving forces of India in the technology space.

## Why ISRO

A fundamental question may arise in any ones mind when hearing about space agencies such as NASA or ISRO — Why do governments spend billions on exploring space when people are starving on the land? It's a good question, to be honest. So, let me answer it for you.

The answer can be divided into three key aspects: technological advancements, practical applications, and national pride. First the technological front. Most of the technologies that are developed for space exploration over time be used by industries to develop newer and innovative products. For example, smaller, lighter cameras with more megapixels were first used in satellites and now they are in everyone's hands. On the Applications front, Space technology applications have significant benefits, including satellite-based telecommunication, internet access, weather forecasting, disaster monitoring, navigation systems, and scientific research, enhancing the quality of human life. The third part is national pride, space power is always associated with pride and confidence of a country. When Chandrayaan-3 was launched none of us had any association with it, but we all took pride, we all celebrated the successful launch. Space exploration inspires us, instils in us pride for the nation and makes the youth of India aspire to greater heights.

Now, we know why ISRO came into existence. I am going to take you through a little history lesson on how ISRO started and how it developed into its present form. Drum roll, please…

## Ignition: The Beginning

Every successful organisation that proved itself against the odds of time owes its inception to one visionary who dedicates their life to a mission. NASA had Dr Wernher von Braun, and for ISRO, it was Dr Vikram A. Sarabhai. ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation)started under the Department of Space (DOS) and was tasked with the development of space technology and its applications for various national needs in 1969. The development of the first satellite took 5 years, and the first rocket launch took 10 years, fostering the growth of some of the best minds India has ever known, including Dr APJ Abdul Kalam, Dr Brahm Prakash, and Udupi Ramachandra Rao, to name a few. This put India in the elite club of countries that had the capability to reach space. 
In the early days, the need for indigenous development of space-grade technology was in every scientist's heart but it was just not possible at that time. ISRO relied on many countries from Russia to France to the USA to get the needed technologies or collaborations for a successful mission.

## Liftoff: The rise of ISRO

Since it came into existence, ISRO focused on developing technology that provided direct developmental benefits, for example, telecommunications and remote sensing satellites that helped both in improving communication facilities and giving direct assistance to India’s farmers. But then as ISRO became a mature organisation, it widened its horizons far into deep space crossing the low-earth orbit.

New and more powerful rocket families were developed along with quantum leaps in the understanding and implementation of communication and probing satellites in the 90s. This put India in the centre spot of technical capabilities as a developing nation. PSLV (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle) played a significant role in advancing India’s space capabilities and enhancing its position in the global space community. Its versatility, reliability, and cost-effectiveness have made it a workhorse for ISRO, contributing to numerous space missions and applications.

The development of Indigenous satellite systems by ISRO made India self-reliable in the Space sector. Some of the satellites developed indigenously include INSAT and GSAT(Indian National Satellite System) for telecommunication, broadcasting, meteorology, and search and rescue operations, IRS (Indian Remote Sensing Satellites) for resource monitoring, environmental management, and disaster management.

The successful mission of Chandrayaan to the moon stays as a hallmark of what ISRO is capable of even with the bare minimum resources available. Chandrayaan mission was responsible for finding the presence of water on the moon along with providing very high-quality images of the moon’s surface.

## Orbit: Manoeuvring the heavens

ISRO is now in a state where the only limit is our mind. The achievements it has achieved in recent times and the missions on the waiting list excite not only us Indians but the whole world in general.

The early 2010s saw the development of India’s indigenous Cryogenic engine and the robust GSLV family of rockets (fondly called the Bahubali Rocket). These rockets are heavy-lift launch vehicles capable of placing satellites way far into space (geostationary position) which only a handful of space powers have achieved. The RLV project is aimed at arming India with Reusable Launch Vehicle capabilities, which only NASA and SpaceX poses.

Also, India is developing its own navigation and positioning system specific to India apart from the Global Positioning System (GPS) by developing and deploying Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System (IRNSS)/NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation) satellites.

India became the 4th nation to reach Mars when ISRO’s Mangalyaan entered the orbit of Mars on November 5, 2013. Mangalyaan’s success was a significant achievement for ISRO and garnered international acclaim. The mission’s cost-effectiveness and success on its maiden attempt made it an exemplary feat in space exploration. Mangalyaan’s findings and data have contributed to global scientific research on Mars, helping to enhance our understanding of the Red Planet.

Then came Chandrayaan-2, which aimed to land a lander and rover on the surface of the moon along with an orbiter that analysed the surface of the moon. But all our dreams came down crashing as the ISRO Command Centre reported the communication loss and eventual crash of the lander and rover due to miscalculations on September 7, 2019.

And now after a bit of history lesson here we are, awaiting the success of Chandrayaan-3, as it inches closer to the moon. With all the valuable lessons from Chandrayaan-2’s setbacks, I sincerely believe this mission will achieve its target.

The future of ISRO is bright, starting with the Privatization of the Space Sector by the Indian government in 2022, which led to a whole new spectrum of industries and people working in the space sector.

The Gaganyaan mission needs an article on its own, is India’s manned space mission, which represents a mammoth project undertaken by ISRO and various organizations, collaborating to achieve human-rated spaceflight and space systems. The Indian Space Station is a future tail project of the Gaganyaan mission aimed at providing a permanent presence of Indians in Space.

India is going into deep space starting with Adithya-L1 and Shukrayaan-1 missions, which are aimed to explore the Sun and Venus respectively. Adithya-L1 is expected to launch on August 26, 2023. So stay tuned for it.

With the spirit of exploration and innovation driving us forward, India’s space program is poised to ascend to greater heights, as it has proved to us all that “Sky is not the limit”.
