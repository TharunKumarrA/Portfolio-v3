I've been thinking about this difference between giving someone the benefit of the doubt and taking them for granted.

Because I think they can look very similar sometimes.

If you're close to me, I don't want you to overthink everything you do around me.

If you don't feel like talking one day, don't talk. If you don't have the energy to reply properly, that's fine. I don't want you sitting there thinking, Will they take this wrongly? Should I explain myself? Are they going to think I'm angry with them?

I mean, that's one of the nice things about being close to someone, right?

You should be able to think, They know me. They'll understand.

There shouldn't be this constant pressure to prove that you care.

But then I think there is another side to the exact same thing.

They'll understand.

So I'll reply later.

They'll understand.

So it doesn't really matter if I cancel again.

They'll understand.

So I don't really have to think about how this might make them feel.

And that's where it gets confusing for me.

Because I want someone to give me the benefit of the doubt, and I want to give them the same. But I don't want either of us to use that understanding as an excuse to take the other person for granted.

Maybe the difference is simply this:

They'll understand, so I don't have to be afraid.

versus

They'll understand, so I don't have to make an effort.

And while thinking about all this, I realised that there is another part of this which is more about me than anyone else.

I tend to make things about me when something feels slightly off.

Someone doesn't talk as much one day.

Are they angry with me?

A reply feels different.

Did I do something wrong?

They're spending more time with someone else.

Am I not the priority anymore?

And I don't really want to do that either.

Because if I'm saying that someone close to me should feel comfortable having an off day around me, then I actually have to give them the space to have an off day.

Not everything needs to mean something.

Sometimes someone doesn't feel like talking.

Sometimes they're tired.

Sometimes they want to spend time with someone else.

Sometimes they have something going on that has absolutely nothing to do with me.

And I think closeness should give us enough trust to not immediately question the entire friendship because of those moments.

You don't judge a relationship from every individual data point. You trust the distribution.

If someone has shown up for me again and again, one bad day doesn't suddenly change that. One late reply doesn't change that. One moment where I wasn't their priority doesn't mean I'm no longer important to them.

But at the same time, I don't think trusting the distribution means ignoring it.

Because if those individual data points keep happening, at some point they become the distribution.

If I'm constantly the person who will understand, constantly the person who can wait, constantly the person who doesn't need the same effort because they know I won't take it badly, then that's different.

And I think this is the balance I'm trying to understand.

I don't want people close to me to feel like they have to constantly reassure me that we're okay.

I want them to have the freedom to be tired, distant, busy, irritated, or just not in the mood without having to worry about what I'll make it mean.

And I want to become better at actually giving them that freedom.

But I also don't want understanding to slowly become an excuse for either of us to stop putting in effort.

Maybe that's all it is.

Giving each other enough benefit of the doubt that the friendship can breathe, without taking each other for granted just because we know it will.
