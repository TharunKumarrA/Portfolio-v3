We often say relationships fall apart because something was missing.

They weren't loving enough.  
They didn't understand us.  
They didn't give us enough attention.  
They didn't make us feel seen.

Sometimes those things are true. People fail each other. Love can become careless, communication can fade, and needs can go unmet.

But I don't think those are the deepest reasons relationships break.

I think they break when both people quietly begin asking the same question:

> “What am I receiving?”

The strongest relationships ask a different one.

> “What can I give?”

That shift changes everything.

Because love was never meant to be an endless negotiation of needs. It was meant to be a continual offering of ourselves.

The healthiest relationships are not built by two people trying to fill their own cups. They're built by two people trying to fill each other's.

There is a subtle difference in language that reflects this.

Most people say, “You're mine.”

There's comfort in those words, but hidden within them is possession. An expectation. A quiet belief that the other person exists to satisfy what we lack.

I prefer something else.

> “I'm yours.”

Not as surrender in the sense of losing yourself, nor as blind obedience, but as an act of service.

It means, “I want to care for you.”  
“I want to make your life lighter.”  
“I want to love you well.”

Imagine two people living by that principle.

Neither is trying to win.  
Neither is keeping score.  
Neither is constantly measuring whether they're getting enough.

Each simply asks, “How can I love this person better today?”

Paradoxically, when both people stop demanding to be filled, they end up overflowing.

Because someone who spends every moment searching for what's missing will never find enough.

But someone who is constantly looking for the good in another will always find more than they expected.

Perhaps that's what love was always meant to be.

Not “You're mine.”

But,

> “I'm yours.”
