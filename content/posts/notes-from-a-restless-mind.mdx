Three years of college flew by in a blur of half-hearted focus and sidewise studying, and now it’s done. Exams are over. But instead of peace, there’s this lingering noise in my head that refuses to be quiet.

It’s 1:30 AM and i’m in train. Sleep doesn’t visit me on time anymore. The nights stretch long, and the afternoons collapse into naps. My body has lost its rhythm, and in that upside-down quiet, my mind does what it does best when left alone too long—it wanders. Picking apart the days gone by, the people passed through, the things felt but never really said.

Lately, it’s been circling around this idea of masks. Not the ones we wear on our faces, but the kind people wear in their personalities. The friendly masks. The agreeable masks. The “I’m just like you” masks. And how they always, always come off—either with a sudden rip that feels like betrayal, or slowly, like watching a statue emerge from stone.

The truth is, no mask lasts forever.

But maybe it’s not always the mask’s fault. Sometimes, it’s our choice to ignore it. To stay in relationships where we only know the mask and not the person. Maybe because it’s easier. Maybe because we don’t want to deal with what’s beneath. But that kind of connection? It never feels whole. It never feels safe.

The harder choice is to acknowledge the mask and try to understand the person behind it. That takes courage. Because what if the truth isn’t better? What if it’s worse? Or unfamiliar?

Still, it’s a choice. Everything in life is.

And that’s the thing about choice—it appears everywhere. There’s always going to be someone more exciting, more beautiful, more whatever. But we still choose who we stay with. We stay not because it’s perfect, but because we chose. It’s not about finding the best thing out there—it’s about finding meaning in what’s ours. Like picking chocolate ice cream. Sure, there might be better flavors. But I still reach for chocolate. And I can always change.

That ability to choose, to change, to evolve—that’s the quiet strength of the human mind. It can shift. It can outgrow its own thoughts and still remain grounded. But in a world full of options and noise, how do we know when a choice is the right one?

Maybe that’s where first principles come in.

The small things. Basic decency. Common sense. The kind of truths that don’t need a spotlight to be seen. When everything feels too complex, too blurred by what-ifs and emotions, these principles anchor us. They don’t guarantee perfection—but they help us avoid regret.

And from that foundation, something else emerges: the understanding that not everything demands a response. Not every wound needs to be matched. Sometimes, it’s not about getting even. Sometimes it’s about choosing not to. Not because we’re weak, but because we’ve outgrown the need for reaction. That’s a kind of maturity too—choosing peace, not out of fear, but from strength.

And this strength can quietly coexist with softness. There’s value in keeping your inner child alive—the one that laughs easily, loves freely, and believes in small magic. But maybe the real balance is pairing that childlike heart with an adult’s clarity. To see people for what they are, to read the silences between their words, to know when to stay—and when to walk away.

And then… sometimes, maybe even that isn’t needed.

Maybe it’s best to stop trying to make sense of everything. Maybe it’s enough to just be.
To feel what comes.
To let it pass.
To breathe.
To live.
