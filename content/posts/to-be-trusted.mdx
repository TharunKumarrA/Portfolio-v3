Most people don’t show who they really are all at once.
They unfold—slowly, over time.
And frankly, no one really believes someone who just entered their life and says,
“Hey, here’s my bio. I’m a kind-hearted person, I care about others, I value honesty, etc.”
It sounds too good to be true—because it usually is.

If someone blindly believes that version right away, it’s only a matter of time before reality hits. And when it does, it can be unsettling.
Real character isn’t in declarations—it’s in duration.

But ironically, we live in a world where people believe a stranger's opinion about someone more easily than the person themselves.
Gossip suddenly feels like evidence.
That’s because, socially, it’s no longer just your word—it’s someone else’s word about you, and that carries more weight.
That’s how reputations are built—and destroyed.

> “Reputation is what others think of you; character is what you really are.” – Thomas Paine

And yet, there’s this moment that happens when you’ve known someone for years.
You hear something about them—something that contradicts everything you know.
Your heart drops. Your mind wonders, “Could it be true?”
But something deeper—quieter—responds: “No. I know them.”
That’s trust.
Not the blind kind, but the kind built through years of quiet, real presence.
That trust becomes a shelter, not just for secrets, but for identity.

> “Trust is built in very small moments.” – Brené Brown

We get to know people in two ways—through what they share, and through what they don’t.
The former gives you insight. The latter gives you assurance.

The way someone chooses silence instead of gossip, the way they don’t react when they could, the way they hold space for others instead of spilling it—
That kind of integrity can’t be faked.
And over time, it tells you more than a hundred stories ever could.

> “Silence is sometimes the best answer.” – Dalai Lama
> “The real test of a person’s character is what they do when no one’s watching.” – John Wooden

Because it’s easy to say the right things.
But to live the right values consistently, when no one’s looking or clapping for you?
That’s trust. And that takes time.

Psychologist Erik Erikson once described the first stage of human development as “Trust vs. Mistrust.”
It’s built not through big moments, but through consistent care and presence.
Carl Jung also believed that people who haven’t faced their own truths are more likely to harm or confuse others—not out of malice, but because they’re ill-informed, even about themselves.

That’s why so many are called “snakes” these days.
They’re not evil—just people who haven’t taken the time to sit with themselves.
So how can they be expected to sit with someone else’s truth? Let alone earn their trust?

Saying “I trust you” doesn’t mean much when it’s said to everyone.
Trust isn’t something you hand out like candy.
It’s the reward for staying consistent, for choosing the same values again and again—even when it’s hard.

> “Until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate.” – Carl Jung

At the end of the day, what matters isn’t how many people know you.
It’s how deeply a few people do.
Not fast friendships, but the kind that have weathered enough storms to stand.

Because trust doesn’t begin with words—it begins with time.
And only time can tell who really meant what they said.

*For those I hold close, and who’ve held me just the same.*
