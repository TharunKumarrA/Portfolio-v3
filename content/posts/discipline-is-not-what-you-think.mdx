When we think of discipline the first thing that comes to our mind are — motivational speeches on how being disciplined will make you successful or how to be tough like David Goggins if you know him. That's not discipline. Those are the side effects of discipline.
And for all mothers out there their favourite phrase is, “You are not Disciplined”. And their solution for it? “Wake up early daily”. A peculiar thing about discipline is that we all know we need it, but we don’t know how to get more of it. Why is this the case?

That's because none of us understand what Disciple is in the first place. If we list out the common definitions of discipline they all go like, “the motivation to do something” or “the willpower to resist” right? But here is the catch, the terms willpower, motivation, habits and discipline are totally different. Let me explain,

Firstly, motivation is like a muscle, after constant usage, it gets tired just like your muscle after a workout. So it won’t help you with being a disciplined person.

Second, habits tough help you with discipline are not the same. Habits are actions that need little to no thinking or decision making such as brushing your teeth after waking up. That’s not discipline That’s autopilot.
Since now we are clear what discipline is not, let’s discuss what discipline actually is.

**Discipline is an Emotion**. It’s not a direct emotion but is strongly connected to the emotion called Resolve which is a positive emotion like happiness. Our saints and sages gave this term a better name — Sangalpa.

Now think about it, when was the last time you were disciplined, it was the time when you resolved to achieve or prove something right? When you think of discipline as an emotion — you can’t simply will it into existence like flipping a switch. Instead, it emerges from a deep well of resolve, a commitment to a purpose that goes beyond momentary desires. So when you need to cultivate discipline, don’t cultivate willpower, don’t cultivate habits, cultivate resolve on a daily basis.

In the words of famous Olympic swimmer Michael Phelps — who has a whopping 28 gold medals puts, Discipline is doing what you need to do even when you feel like not doing it.

Now that we have a clear understanding of what discipline is, let’s see its importance in modern times, especially for folks like us — the Knowledge workers, people who use their brain more than their body as coined by Peter Drucker.

In modern times being disciplined has become a real challenge as all the products we use are meant for comfort such as delivery apps that give food right to the doorstep or cars that we take just to go to the next street. In the digital space also, most of the apps and websites keep us engaged with their product by hooking us to short-form content and catchy notifications thus breaking our focus and distracting us from the work that needs to be done. 
Being a disciplined person has so many benefits for all of us.

When you cultivate discipline in your life, you prioritise the things that matter in your life, instead of attacking what comes in your day without a plan, Like getting up early or playing football daily. Since you have your priorities set right, you will now have more free time and thus be more mindful about what you can do with your day, such as working on a side project you have been postponing for a long time or spending time with your kids or even taking a walk in nature. Discipline gives you control over your time, actions and overall life.

As famous US Navy seal and Author, Jocko Willink puts it beautifully, “Discipline = Freedom”
