A hometown is something special. Someone who grew up there knows every street, every shortcut, every crack in the walls of their house. But for a passerby? It’s just another place on the map.

Friends, in a way, are like that too. Some are just people you pass by on the street. Some roam with you through parts of your city. But only a few ever step inside your house. And even then, they may never be as comfortable there as they are in their own homes, for it is you who knows which door lock jams, which floorboard creaks, where your favorite mug is kept.

Imagine for a moment that you’re a traveller. You love exploring the world, soaking in different cultures, living a life full of adventure.

One day, you’re crossing a small town in the foothills of the Himalayas. Sounds dreamy, right? But nothing there catches your attention. You skim past, noticing only a few shepherds and some people moving about. To you, it’s just a nameless stop.

Then you reach another town. This one feels alive. You wander through its bustling market, visit its monastery, chat with the locals, maybe even buy something as a keepsake. You leave with memories. Compared to the first town, you now know this place a little better. Yet you’re still just a tourist. For the people who live there, it’s home. For they know every hidden alley, every shopkeeper’s mood, every festival’s story.

And then there’s your own hometown. No matter how far you travel, nothing feels quite like it. You grew up there, picking up friends along the way. Some you only shared school lunches with. Some walked home with you. Some helped you during tuition. Others became closer—the ones you played games with every evening, celebrated Diwali with, the ones your mom welcomed inside. Among them, a few became family. The real OGs. They knew your house so well they could tell your dad’s bike just by its sound, because they had spent enough evenings waiting outside your gate, enough mornings running in to call you out to play.

But still, no matter how close they were, only you truly knew your home. You knew where the walls had cracked, which chair made that weird squeaky noise, which plant in the garden was your favorite.

Now replace this hometown with you, your inner self. Every person you meet in life is just passing through your “town.” Some will only ever stay at the outer roads, exchanging small talk. Some will explore more, share laughter, memories, a part of your life. A few will get closer, like neighbors in your street—they’ll feel comfortable around you, understand parts of you others don’t.

And then there are the rare few who become family. The ones who might know your habits, quirks, fears, dreams. They’ll know not to spill coffee near your favorite rug, just like someone who’s been to your home many times. Maybe they’ll become your closest friend. Maybe even your life partner. For they will have walked through more rooms of your “house” than anyone else.

But here’s the truth. No one, not even them, will ever know your entire “home” the way you do. And that’s okay. Just as every town has its own stories, every person carries a life as vast and layered as yours. Every stranger you meet is someone else’s hometown. Someone else’s entire world.

So treat people gently. Understand that everyone is more than what we see on the surface. And when it comes to friendships and relationships, it’s okay if not everyone becomes a “homie.” Some people are meant to share a coffee, some a festival, and a rare few will walk into the deepest parts of your “house.”

And for yourself? Be kind. You are the only one who truly knows every corner of your own home. So take care of it. Because at the end of every adventure, you always return to your hometown—you.
