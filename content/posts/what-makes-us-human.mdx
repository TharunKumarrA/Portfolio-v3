We as humans have always been fascinated by the fact that we are unique compared to our animal friends — to be able to go beyond food, shelter and reproduction. It feels like the whole world with all its mountains, rivers, valleys, forests and animals is here for the sake of us. But at the same time, we feel insignificant, our problems seem nothing when faced with the spectacles of nature. Why are humans so complicated? Why is our culture so messy? Why can't we be just like any other animal? Why are we different? In this post let's explore the answers to these questions.

At the biological level, humans are distinct from other mammals in the aspects of — the brain itself and the spine structure.

- The human brain has a new addition — something extraordinary — that no other animal brain possesses: the Prefrontal cortex. This part of the brain is responsible for reasoning and planning. As neuroscientist Andrew Huberman stated in one of his podcasts, “The Prefrontal cortex is the part of the brain that says NO when needed to” (as opposed to instinctive reaction) which is generally referred to as Thinking.

- The brain: body weight ratio for humans is 1:40 while it's around 1:180 for mammals and around 1:220 for birds.

- The general spine structure of mammals is horizontal (quadrupedal), while we humans have an erect spine (bipedal) so as to balance our heavy brains.

Now, the biological difference we saw above can be explained by the theory of evolution. The intellectual difference is what makes us so different from all other mammals. So, did God bless humans with the ability the think? The answer is not related to God at all but has to do with how a human is born and grown.

Let's take the example of 2 animals — the Sea Turtle and the Giraffe. Sea Turtles hatch alone without the mother Turtle and wander in the vast ocean all alone (if they survive the beach escaping all the predators) for around a decade until they mature. Also, they know when to leave the nest to reach the ocean, and they can find water with the help of some cues such as the slope of the beach or natural ocean light — all just a few hours after they are born[1]. Now, coming to our tall friend Giraffe, within hours of leaving the safety of their mum’s womb, young giraffes can stand, feed, and walk, which is particularly important when they need to follow their herd away from the threat of ferocious carnivores[2]. Also, Giraffe offsprings are fully developed and huge when they are born.

The above-seen are the best examples of how animal infants develop instincts and knowledge when they are in the mother's womb itself. The instinct of sea turtle hatchlings to run towards water and the ability of giraffe offspring to stand within an hour after birth are due to the high development of the animal's brain and body inside the womb. Generally, animals tend to have a relatively low childhood as most of the developments happened inside the womb.

Now, if we see a newborn human baby, it will be the most unfit to survive in the world. It's not an exaggeration, let me explain. When born, babies don't have proper eyesight, have limbs that will break with the lightest force and more importantly a neck that can’t even bear its own head's weight. It will take 1 year for a human infant to hold its own head up and stand, 3 years to run properly and 5 years to have proper communication with fellow humans. Generally, a newborn’s head is about half the baby’s body length in cm plus 10 cm. But only the lower portions of the nervous system (the spinal cord and brain stem) are very well developed, whereas the higher regions (the limbic system and cerebral cortex) are still rather primitive.

So, most of the frontal part of the brain is developed outside the mother's womb. This directly implies the brain tries to understand many things in its surroundings (outside the sphere of survival too). For example, a baby still doesn't know about the world it's in, so it can question let's say — what the Moon is or why the sky is blue. This can’t be done by any other animals because their brains are already hard-wired in certain ways before birth and have little development afterwards[3].

The above factor is the main reason humans changed the world in ways never happened before in the history of the earth. When humans learnt how to handle fire or learnt how to use tools, the children who grew up also learnt how to use them as their brains were still growing. This paved the way for the brain to learn even after childhood. This means the tools and ideas created by one set of humans can be passed not only to other tribes but also to the next generations.

We claim we are not wild, and that we are modern humans because, from the day we were born, we have been growing up in surroundings that are different from the wild forests. Our brains were developed in this new surrounding, understanding what’s around it. That's why not all modern humans can survive in the wild. They just don't know the surroundings and the survival means.

The fact that we learn so much about the world around us after being born and for a long period of time also means, different people will have different experiences, thus creating humans with different thinking, different ideologies, and different practices — paving the way for all the cultures, languages and civilisations present in the world.

In conclusion, all these technological advancements, control of food and water, creation of systems, and evolution of civilisations, kingdoms and nations are all because of one reason — when born we can't survive on our own and our brains need to develop for a long long time.
