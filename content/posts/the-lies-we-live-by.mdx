There is this interesting phenomenon that happens with prison inmates who have been in jail for a long time. When they come out into the normal world after serving their time, they find it difficult to adjust to life outside.

On the surface level, it’s understandable. A lot of things have changed. Technology has moved forward, people have moved on, and the world they once knew is no longer the same. But there is also a psychological aspect that many hardly notice.

There is evidence of former inmates committing crimes again simply to get back into prison when they cannot cope with life outside.

At first, this feels counterintuitive. Why would someone choose prison over freedom?

The answer lies in something deeper.

Prison is restrictive, but it is also predictable. Every day follows a structure. The rules are known. The consequences are familiar. There is certainty in confinement.

Freedom, on the other hand, comes with uncertainty. It demands choices. It demands responsibility. It comes with the possibility of failure.

This is interesting because it reveals something about human nature. They become so used to and comfortable with prison life not because it is pleasant, but because it is certain. It is predictable. It is safe.

The mind, if conditioned to hardships or problems for a prolonged period of time, will find comfort in an alternate reality that it constructs for itself just to feel protected and certain when the problem is not being faced in reality.

Reality is painful. The lie is comfortable.

Given enough time, the mind chooses comfort.

We may not be prisoners in the literal sense, but we often do something surprisingly similar.

Many of us go through life without truly understanding ourselves or making decisions for ourselves because it is hard. It demands effort. It demands honesty. It asks us to sit with uncertainty, with risk, and with the possibility that we may fail.

Our mind is smart. It is always trying to find the shortest, quickest, and easiest way to solve a problem. Sometimes that solution is not solving the problem at all. Sometimes it is pretending the problem is not there. Sometimes it is constructing a story around ourselves that makes reality easier to live with.

Given enough time, this process of lying to ourselves instead of confronting the issue moves into the subconscious. What starts as a defense mechanism slowly becomes part of our identity.

And eventually, we become oblivious to our true nature.

Who we think we are is not who we are.

Now this is where it becomes harmful, both to us and to the people around us. Our actions and intentions stop aligning. Our feelings and reality begin to clash. The image we have of ourselves drifts further and further away from the truth.

One thing we cannot change is the fundamental nature of being human.

Our emotions do not care about the stories we tell ourselves.

Our values do not care about the stories we tell ourselves.

Our fears, insecurities, defense mechanisms, and ego do not care about the stories we tell ourselves.

They reveal themselves regardless.

They show up in our actions. They show up in our relationships. They show up in the choices we make when nobody is watching.

And a person who is in conflict with himself cannot make peace with others.

The only solution to this is difficult for many because it is not easy and it is not comfortable.

It is to sit with reality.

To face the challenge.

To take the risk.

To accept the uncertainty.

That’s it.

A bird can become comfortable in its cage.

It knows every corner.

It knows where the food comes from.

It knows it is safe.

The cage may even be made of gold.

But it is still a cage.

The bird can convince itself that the cage is the entire world. It can spend years becoming comfortable in it. It can defend it. It can mistake familiarity for freedom.

But safety was never the purpose.

The bird was made to fly.

To face the winds.

To cross oceans.

To explore skies it has never seen before.

If the bird believes the cage is the world, then I am sorry for it.

Because it possesses the ability to soar through the skies and yet chooses the certainty of a few metal bars.

The cage offers certainty.

Flight offers possibility.

And sometimes, the hardest thing a living being can do is leave behind what is comfortable for what it was truly meant to become.
