Hell and heaven, in some religious contexts, are seen as places for the afterlife. But if we set that aside for a moment, what if they’re more about our experiences in the here and now? Times when we feel defeated, lost, or stuck — that’s when hell feels real. And times when we feel joy, connection, and peace — that's when heaven seems close.

When do we feel defeated, or suffer, or experience unpleasantness? It’s usually over something or someone, right? We feel defeated when we fail to attain our dreams or goals. We suffer the loss of someone close. We feel unpleasant when something bad happens in our life. The common thread in all of this is that what feels like hell is attached to an object, person, or goal. A breakup, an unmet dream, a loss—it’s all linked to something external.

Now, what makes us feel happy or joyful? It’s not always easy to capture all the little moments that bring us happiness, but there’s some: being with loved ones, trying something new, eating our favorite food, or riding our bike to a new place. On the surface, these are tied to things, people, or experiences. But if we dig a little deeper, it’s more than that. It’s the journey, the moment we’re in. It’s the company we keep, the breeze we feel, the tight hug that brings comfort. That’s when happiness isn’t just because of things or being there; it’s because of truly being in the moment.

At the end of the day, it’s all in the mind. Our perceptions shape our experiences, turning any situation, any person, or any place into either hell or heaven. The same mind that builds our prisons also holds the key to set us free.
As much as life throws us into difficult situations, it is ultimately we who hold the key out.

Hell is a place where we can get stuck because it’s a place we create in our minds. It’s why people get caught in the past—whether from breakups, traumas, or bad experiences. That’s why bad moments feel eternal. It feels like it’s going nowhere, but it’s a place you’ve built. And it’s your responsibility to move out of it.

For the same reasons, heaven is not a constant state we can stay in. It’s not a place—it’s a journey. In a journey, you feel amazed, tired, happy, hopeful, lost, curious, fatigued, not because the journey is hard, but because it’s all part of it. The best part? The journey is never over. Heaven isn’t a destination; it’s an experience you create as you move through life. And as you continue your journey, you’ll find yourself in heaven more often than you realize, because

> “this life is one hell of a place and heaven of a journey.”
